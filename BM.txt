GNN:
- each node represents a player, with associated attributes as values in each dimension. An edge between two players mean they are able to see the other player, their attributes are related. So, on the next layer of the network, the node's new adjusted position is altered slightly, based on this relationship

GNN would be very useful at predicting a players temporary attributes for a given game. E.g. Tatum has base 3pt shot of 90, but with Jaylen brown out, high morale, and playing against the wizards (bad perimeter D), this rating would be higher for this game, maybe a 92. This would impact the possession by possession simulation, giving him a higher percentage for every 3pt shot the engine allocates for him.

ARCHITECTURE:

1. Autoencoder (VAE)
- take historical data relative to minutes played and league averages for each category
- gives an output vector with probabilities for each attribute, with a standard deviation for natural error

2. Graph Neural Network 
- two separate GNNs
- 1. within team relationships, learn how players perform with each other (chemistry, spacing, fit)
- 2. Matchup graph of relationships between player and likely matchup, gives a probability for this to allow calculation for the impact of matchups
- output of both of these networks result in "temporary attribute adjustments" from base attributes, which influence gameplay based on the factors mentioned, as well as others such as current morale, recent form etc. These temporary attributes are carried into next game
